**Plain language summary**

This summary is for policymakers.

Average BMI has increased across 
low-, middle-, and high-income 
adults since 1999. The increase 
has been different for each 
income group and across survey 
years.

The results show a pattern over 
time. They do not prove that 
income causes changes in BMI, or 
that one income group caused 
changes in another.

Policymakers should use this 
information to guide public 
health programs and resources. 
Next, researchers should study 
every survey cycle from 1999 to 
2023 while accounting for age, 
sex, race and ethnicity, and 
other factors that may affect 
BMI. This could lead to a 
discussion about which policies 
and programs may help all income 
groups.
